Why this page exists

Public searches about my professional background may bring up reports concerning criminal proceedings in Russia and a separate civil action filed in the United States. I am making the relevant court records and my position available here so that business counterparties and other readers can distinguish established judicial findings, reported allegations and statements made by me or my counsel.

This page is intended to provide documentary context, not to replace independent due diligence.

Russian proceedings and my position

I was detained in Russia in June 2021 in connection with criminal proceedings involving allegations of fraud and participation in a criminal community. In September 2023, a Russian court convicted me following an admission of guilt and a cooperation agreement.

I dispute the fairness of those proceedings and maintain that my admission was made under pressure.

Contemporaneous international reporting

On 18 April 2022, The Jerusalem Post reported that Israeli consular officials had not been permitted to visit me, quoting confirmation from Israel’s Foreign Ministry.

Against the background of Russia’s demand concerning ownership of the Alexander Courtyard in Jerusalem, the article also raised the question of whether Moscow might use my detention as negotiating leverage. This was a possibility raised in the reporting, rather than an established finding about the reasons for my detention.

My account of pressure and loss of assets

My own account is that I faced repeated pressure and attempts to use my situation for purposes extending beyond the criminal proceedings. I believe that obtaining access to my money ultimately became a principal motive.

I also maintain that funds were transferred from my personal cryptocurrency wallets without my authorisation and that the affected wallets were emptied. These included hot wallets and wallets whose private keys I had kept in a depository together with documents.

The loss of these assets and the circumstances in which access to the wallets was obtained form part of the wider events for which I am seeking independent assessment. Documents and police reports were submitted in the United Kingdom and also in the Cayman Islands on behalf of BR Capital and may be provided as supporting evidence.

Wider context on Russian criminal justice

In his 2013 report on the Russian Federation, the Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights documented concerns about prosecutorial bias and pressure on judges. He also warned that high conviction rates, strict sentencing and low public confidence could lead even innocent defendants to plead guilty.

Read the Council of Europe Commissioner's report — paragraphs 33 and 58–61

An October 2023 country-information report by the European Union Agency for Asylum (EUAA), citing Amnesty International, reported that in 2022 only 0.4% of defendants were acquitted or had criminal proceedings terminated on rehabilitating grounds. This is a combined measure of those outcomes, not the percentage of convictions overturned on appeal.

Assessment by Defense Counsel

Anatoly Kleymenov — Advocate and Russian-Law Expert

Anatoly Kleymenov is a Russian-qualified advocate with more than 30 years of experience in criminal defense, including complex economic-crime cases. His practice includes INTERPOL and extradition matters, and he has successfully secured the deletion of client data from INTERPOL's files.

He holds a Candidate of Legal Sciences degree and has provided Russian-law expertise in proceedings before courts in Cyprus and the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York. He personally represented Rezo (Revaz) Shmertz in the Russian criminal proceedings.

Additional context highlighted by defense counsel

In further comments on my case, Mr. Kleymenov drew attention to reporting concerning proceedings against individuals whom I did not know and with whom I had no personal connection. These individuals had been portrayed as belonging to a group with which I was also sought to be associated. According to the reporting, they continued to resist the system to the end and received sentences ranging from eight to twenty-one years. The defendants had not admitted guilt.

Mr. Kleymenov emphasised their continued denial of guilt alongside these outcomes as relevant context for his assessment of the pressures surrounding my own plea. European Court of Human Rights judgment

On 23 November 2023, the European Court of Human Rights delivered its final judgment in Mazanov and Others v. Russia. My application is listed as Revaz SHMERTS, no. 2976/22, entry 10 in the appendix.

In relation to my application, the Court found violations of Article 5 §§ 1 and 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights. The findings concerned an unrecorded period of detention on 8 June 2021 and the excessive length of my pre-trial detention, which remained ongoing as of 16 September 2022. The appendix identifies insufficient grounds for continued detention and failure to consider alternatives. The Court awarded EUR 3,900.

Following Russia’s expulsion from the Council of Europe in March 2022 following Ukraine invasion), it ceased to be a party to the European Convention on Human Rights on 16 September 2022. The European Court of Human Rights retained jurisdiction over alleged violations relating to acts or omissions occurring up to that date.

My conviction was delivered in September 2023, after that cutoff. The judgment discussed here concerns my earlier detention; it no more could determine the fairness of the later conviction or whether my admission of guilt was obtained under pressure.

Separate U.S. civil proceedings

In March 2025, a private claimant filed Rea v. Shmertz et al. in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Florida (No. 1:25-cv-21360). A public docket entry dated 5 May 2025 records dismissal of the case without prejudice, following an earlier order addressing deficiencies in the complaint.

This action was a private civil lawsuit, not a U.S. criminal prosecution. The complaint's assertions were not judicial findings, and the dismissal was not a trial determination that every allegation was false.
